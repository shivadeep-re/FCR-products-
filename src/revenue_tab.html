How battery wear cost changes the winner

FCR-N cycles the battery about 1 time a day on a 2 h battery, FCR-D almost never. The more each MWh of cycling costs, the better FCR-D looks.

Is this realistic? Market benchmarks

  • Clean Horizon's index for a 2-hour battery in Sweden: €164k/MW/year in June 2026 and €126k/MW/year in July 2026 (−20% after FCR and mFRR capacity prices fell about 30%). That is about €10–14k per MW per month, optimised across FCR, mFRR and spot trading. Clean Horizon, ESS News
  • Montel (2025): a 1 MW / 2 MWh battery in Sweden can earn about €100k/MW/year. Montel
  • Svenska kraftnät reported an average FCR-N price of €29.9/MW per hour for October 2025, with August and September 2025 at similar levels. Svenska kraftnät
  • FCR-D prices collapsed in 2024 as prequalified battery capacity (about 600 MW) overtook demand (below 550 MW). Montel, Flower

What this model leaves out

  • It assumes every bid is accepted at the hourly price and that the battery does not move the price itself.
  • Grid fees on the auxiliary supply, insurance, O&M, delivery penalties and capital costs are not included, so net is an operating margin, not profit.
  • Energy activated for FCR is valued at spot; the actual settlement may use imbalance prices.
  • One month of prices: summer months are usually lower than winter, and prices swing a lot from month to month.
